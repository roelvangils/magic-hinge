Craig mode. Activated.

Give your desktop its keynote moment. Watch your windows turn to frosted glass as the lid moves. Dramatic presentation skills sold separately.

02 / JUST ONE MORE TIME

Open. Close. Repeat.

Find your favourite angle in the 3D simulator. Drag the laptop, move the slider, or use the arrow keys. A perfectly good way to get absolutely nothing done.

03 / CALL IT A DAY

Close it. Duo Style.

Save a little magic for the end of the day. Enable the closing effect, then shut the lid and enjoy your final flourish. Leaving early has never looked so good.

BEFORE YOU BEND

System requirements

Here’s what you need to get started.

Apple silicon
A Mac with an Apple M-series chip. Intel Macs aren’t supported.
macOS 14.2 or later
This is the minimum version. Testing on macOS 14.2 itself is still pending during the public beta.
A readable hinge sensor
Automatic lid control requires a MacBook with a sensor the app can read. Magic Hinge checks your Mac when it starts; support isn’t guaranteed for every model. The simulator works without a sensor.
Screen Recording access
Needed to animate your real desktop. You can skip permission and use the built-in example image.
An internet connection to get set up
3D models download directly from Apple the first time you use them. Downloaded models stay available offline.

MAKE YOURSELF AT HOME

One small app.
Two ways to install.

Open the disk image and drag Magic Hinge into Applications. Or use Homebrew:

brew install --cask roelvangils/tap/magic-hinge

The app speaks English and Dutch.

This is an early public beta. Hardware, permissions and VoiceOver testing is still in progress. Report a bug on GitHub.

Made to feel light.

The effect clears on sleep, screen lock and sensor loss. It does not prevent sleep or draw over the secure login screen. Update checks are optional; automatic downloads and installation are off.
